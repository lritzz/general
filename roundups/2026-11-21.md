### 1. A Trump housing official jailed for driving 109 mph had defended speeding on Reddit
https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/20/politics/video/ebof-kfile-aaron-kofsky-reckless-driving-reddit
- CNN KFile (Nov 20): Aaron Kofsky, a senior deputy to FHFA Director Bill Pulte, was sentenced to jail time after being clocked at 109 mph on I-66. He had posted on Reddit that "speed is not inherently dangerous."

### 2. Germany's "Gen Z regelt" movement held a second day of nationwide protests in 73 cities
https://www.aa.com.tr/en/europe/won-t-just-sit-back-germany-s-gen-z-mobilizes-over-conscription-living-costs-climate/4131209
- Students protested on Nov 20 against military conscription, social-spending cuts and surveillance. ZDF reported nearly 90,000 participants, mostly peaceful, with rioting in Frankfurt and Hamburg ([ZDF liveblog](https://zdfheute.de/politik/deutschland/gen-z-proteste-schulpolitik-militaerdienst-liveblog-100.html)).
- It's part of a wave of Gen Z protests across Europe (France, Italy, Spain, Serbia).
- **Why it fits:** It's a big Gen Z mobilization that US outlets have mostly not covered. It also gives a useful comparison to US youth organizing after the midterms.

### 3. Ken Paxton drops his ActBlue lawsuit as his term winds down
https://dallasnews.com/news/politics/article/texas-attorney-general-ken-paxton-drops-actblue-22542453.php
- On Nov 20, the outgoing Texas AG dismissed his suit against Democrats' main small-dollar fundraising platform. It's the latest case his office has dropped since Democrat Nathan Johnson won the AG race.
- **Why it fits:** It touches political fundraising and political tech, and it's one of the first concrete ways the midterm results are reshaping the fight over ActBlue and WinRed.
